## Notes on Structure

### Productive conceptual roots

Several roots recur across the lexicon and help give the language coherence.

- `val` often signals negation, absence, loss, or estrangement
- `kesh` often relates to body, flesh, kinship, or personhood
- `sacht` often carries ideas of continuation, size, elevation, or status
- `nocht` often signals becoming, change, or structural transformation
- `tsoum` often appears in words linked to existence, life-state, or lived condition

### Fossilized compounds

Some words clearly preserve older internal structures while now functioning as whole lexical items. Examples include:

- `ruvalnacht` — omen
- `sikeshi` — tribe
- `raknacht` — journey
- `valkecht` — silence
- `tso'koa` — ritualized ruin-form

### Sound character

The language favors clustered, forceful sounds such as:

- `ts`
- `ch`
- `tch`
- `cht`

This gives it a dense, ritual, and weathered sound.

## Compound Evaluation Rules

The Ancient Tongue resolves compounds by **right-branching evaluation**.

This means a compound of the form:

`A B C D`

is interpreted as:

`A(B(C(D)))`

The **final element is the substrate or base concept**, and each preceding element applies a force, state, or modifier to the structure on its right.

Examples:

- `linru` → `lin(ru)` → vanished knowledge → forget
- `valkecht` → `val(kecht)` → negated hearing → silence
- `ruvalnacht` can be understood as a lexicalized compound whose internal grouping has become fixed over time

### Binding and lexical priority

Compound evaluation follows this order:

1. **Lexicalized compounds resolve first**
2. **Directional bindings with `'s` resolve next**
3. **Remaining compounds resolve right to left**
4. **Separate written words remain phrases, not compounds**

So if a compound already exists as a recognized lexical unit, it is treated as a single block before further evaluation.

### Direction marker

The marker `'s` functions as a directional or applicative linker.

A form like:

`A'sB`

means roughly:

`A applied toward B`

This allows internal grouping inside larger compounds.

## Erosion Rules

Over time, compounds in the Ancient Tongue can erode phonetically and morphologically. This creates shorter living forms from older conceptual stacks.

### 1. Vowel collision reduction

When vowels meet across a compound boundary, one may disappear.

`V + V → V`

### 2. Repeated root compression

Repeated roots may partially collapse.

`X + X → X / reduced X`

This helps explain compact kinship forms.

### 3. Direction marker erosion

The linker `'s` may disappear in speech over time.

`A'sB → AB`

### 4. Weak vowel reduction

Unstressed vowels may reduce toward `i` or disappear.

### 5. Cluster simplification

Dense consonant clusters may simplify in speech.

### 6. Long compound collapse

Very long compounds tend to compress in the middle over generations.

This is how older conceptual compounds can survive in shortened modern forms.

### Example: tribe

An older form such as:

`sikei + kesh + 's + kesh`

can erode over time into:

`sikeshi`

So the modern form may preserve only part of the older visible structure while still carrying its historical meaning.

---

## Historical Layering

The language now operates across multiple layers at once:

1. **Primitive roots**
2. **Conceptual compounds**
3. **Bound constructions using `'s`**
4. **Eroded historical forms**

This means not every modern lexical item remains transparently analyzable on the surface, even when its older structure is known.

## Short Sample

```text
Tso'koa, Tso'koa
Neali micht tealeh
Tso'koa, Tso'koa
Neali tso nach tealeh
```

A common interpretive rendering of this opening would be:

> Bring ruin, bring ruin.  
> Our lives are yours.  
> Bring ruin, bring ruin.  
> We give you our future.

## Current State

The Ancient Tongue already presents itself as:

- phonetically coherent
- morphologically patterned
- conceptually dense
- culturally mythic rather than mundane

It reads less like a modern utilitarian language and more like a language shaped by ritual, memory, hierarchy, survival, and inherited symbolic thought.
